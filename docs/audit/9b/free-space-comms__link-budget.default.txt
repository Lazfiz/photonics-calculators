Model (Friis transmission)

P_r = P_t + G_t + G_r − 20 log₁₀(4πR/λ) − γ_atm·R − L_misc [dB]

The transmitter is a Gaussian beam of full 1/e² divergence θ, so its on-axis gain is 8/(θ/2)². The receiver collects the plane wave over its aperture: G_r = (πD/λ)². The free-space path loss and the two gains together are the beam spreading: for a small receiver the product equals 2a²/w(R)², the share of a Gaussian beam of radius w = θR/2 that falls on an aperture of radius a. Closer in, or for a large receiver, the card shows the exact capture 1 − exp(−2a²/w(R)²) with w(R) = w₀√(1 + (R/z_R)²). Atmospheric attenuation is per kilometre: take it from the fog, rain or snow calculators. With θ fixed, λ cancels in P_r (G_t·G_r/FSPL = D²/(2θ²R²) for a small receiver); it matters only through the atmospheric attenuation, which you enter for your wavelength, and the diffraction-limited waist.

Not modelled: turbulence fading, pointing jitter statistics and background noise. The sensitivity is the receiver's power at the target bit error rate.

MODEL AND REFERENCES

Textbook approximation. A standard approximation from the literature (for example paraxial, plane-wave or Gaussian-beam), valid within the range the page states. About the tiers

Budget table from Friis (G_t = 8/θ², θ half-angle; G_r = (πD/λ)²); the received power is the exact Gaussian capture 1 − exp(−2a²/w²), which Friis matches far out. Atmosphere in dB/km; no turbulence.
