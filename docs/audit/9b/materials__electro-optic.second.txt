Model

Δn = −½ n³ r E

Longitudinal KDP-type cell: the field along the optic axis makes the x′ and y′ axes (at 45° to x, y) differ by n_o³r₆₃E, so the retardation is Γ = 2πn_o³r₆₃V/λ and V_π = λ/(2n_o³r₆₃). A beam polarized along x′ alone sees half that change, so a phase modulator needs twice the voltage.

Transverse LiNbO₃ modulator: the field along z across a gap d acts over the length L, so V_π scales as d/L. The z-polarized beam uses r₃₃; the z–o retardation uses n_e³r₃₃ − n_o³r₁₃ and adds to the crystal's natural birefringence, which a real modulator compensates.

KTP (biaxial, mm2) in the same geometry with light along x: the z polarization uses n_z³r₃₃ and the y polarization n_y³r₂₃, so the retardation uses n_z³r₃₃ − n_y³r₂₃. Its r₃₃ and r₂₃ are 633 nm values.

r is taken at its reference wavelength (633 nm for LiNbO₃ and KTP) and only n follows the Sellmeier fit, so V_π far from it is approximate. LiTaO₃ is the exception: its unclamped r₃₃ and r₁₃ were measured at 632.8, 980, 1330 and 1558 nm and are interpolated between them (r₃₃ falls from 30.5 to 27.4 pm/V), so its V_π holds over that range only. LiNbO₃'s r₃₃ and r₁₃ are the clamped (high-frequency) values; below the acoustic resonances the unclamped ones are a few per cent larger. KTP's r₃₃ and r₂₃ are the unclamped (low-frequency) values; vendor tables give 35.0 and 13.8 pm/V clamped.

LiTaO₃ (3m) works like LiNbO₃ but its birefringence is small and of the other sign (n_e − n_o ≈ +0.004), so the natural retardation of a transverse modulator is about 20× smaller. Its coefficients are the unclamped (low-frequency) values of Casson et al., scaled to the literature r₃₃ = 30.5 pm/V at 632.8 nm; their r₁₃ there is 7.62 pm/V against 8.4 in the older interferometric value. Its permittivity is not tabulated here.

BaTiO₃ (4mm) uses the same z-field geometry with n_e³r₃₃ − n_o³r₁₃, for a poled single-domain crystal at 633 nm and 23 °C. Its piezoelectric part is large: clamped r₃₃ is 40.6 against 105 pm/V unclamped, so V_π rises about 3× above the first acoustic resonance (≈ 0.1 MHz for a 2 cm crystal). Its largest coefficient, r₄₂ = 1300 pm/V (clamped 730), needs a field across the c-axis and is not modelled; n_e and r also change strongly with temperature.

BSO (Bi₁₂SiO₂₀, cubic 23) is isotropic until a field is applied. With the field along [110] and the light along [1̄10], the induced axes lie at ±45° between [110] and [001] and differ by n³r₄₁E, so V_π = λd/(2n³r₄₁L). The crystal is also optically active: it rotates the polarization by ρL (21 °/mm at 633 nm), and the rotation adds in quadrature to the induced birefringence (Efremidis et al. 2009, eqs. 8–10). With the analyzer crossed to the zero-field output, the transmission becomes (cos ρL·ρL·S − sin ρL·cos Δ)² + cos²ρL·(Γ/2)²S², Δ = √((Γ/2)² + (ρL)²), S = sin Δ/Δ, which falls short of 1 at V_π once ρL is large: keep ρL small or compensate it. r₄₁, n and ρ are Efremidis et al.'s, measured with a dc field (unclamped) from 500 to 800 nm: r₄₁ falls from 4.1 to 2.0 pm/V, below the older single-wavelength values in the red (3.8–5.0 pm/V at 633–850 nm).

MODEL AND REFERENCES

Textbook approximation. A standard approximation from the literature (for example paraxial, plane-wave or Gaussian-beam), valid within the range the page states. About the tiers

Principal-axis geometries with r fixed at its reference wavelength; only n follows the Sellmeier fits (Zernike KDP, Zelmon LiNbO₃, Kato KTP, Wemple BaTiO₃ over 0.4–0.7 µm), and KD*P and ADP use one published index. LiTaO₃'s unclamped r₃₃ and r₁₃ are Casson et al.'s at 632.8, 980, 1330 and 1558 nm (scaled to r₃₃ = 30.5 pm/V at 632.8 nm), interpolated linearly, with Bond's tabulated indices; it is offered over 632.8–1558 nm only. LiNbO₃ uses clamped (high-frequency) coefficients; KTP's 633 nm r₃₃ and r₂₃ are low-frequency measurements whose clamping the source doesn't state. BaTiO₃ comes unclamped (r₃₃ 105, r₁₃ 8 pm/V) and clamped (40.6, 10.2); r₄₂ = 1300 (clamped 730) pm/V needs a field across c and is not modelled. The z–o retardation of a transverse modulator adds to the natural birefringence. BSO (field along [110]): Efremidis et al.'s r₄₁, n and optical activity at 500–800 nm (Fig. 3, interpolated; below 560 nm r₄₁ may read low from screening), with the rotation in the crossed-polarizer transmission.
